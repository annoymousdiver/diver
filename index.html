Abstract

Scaling robot data and model capacity has improved Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, but further progress is constrained by the high cost and limited scalability of robotic data. Verifier-guided test-time scaling offers a complementary path by using additional inference-time computation to select among multiple candidate actions. Existing classification-based verifiers are trained from trajectory-level outcomes while weighting all visited states equally. However, candidate selection matters primarily at sparse decision-critical states, where different actions can lead to different outcomes, rather than at the routine states that dominate a trajectory. We therefore propose DIVER, a decision-criticality-weighted verifier that uses the dispersion of candidate action-expert representations as a proxy for decision criticality and reweights verifier training toward these high-impact states. Across LIBERO, RoboCasa, and a real Franka Research 3 platform, our decision-weighted verifier achieves state-of-the-art performance with negligible inference overhead.

s

We propose DIVER, a decision-criticality-weighted verifier for VLA test-time scaling.

  • Identify where decisions matter. We estimate decision criticality from the variance of sampled action representations, without additional rollouts or step-level supervision.
  • Focus verifier learning on critical states. DIVER places greater training emphasis on sparse states where candidate actions diverge and selection is more consequential.
  • Improve test-time action selection efficiently. DIVER consistently improves Best-of-N VLA performance across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks with negligible verification overhead.

Quantitative Results

We evaluate DIVER on two simulated manipulation environments, LIBERO-Long and RoboCasa, as well as a real-world Franka Research 3 robot, using both π0 and π0.5. Across these settings, DiVeR consistently improves verifier-guided Best-of-N action selection over competitive baselines. We further study candidate scaling, component ablations, unseen-task generalization, qualitative action selection, and inference latency, showing that the gains remain robust while verifier overhead is negligible.

Qualitative Results


We qualitatively compare the base VLA policy with DIVER on a Franka Research 3 robot. Starting from the same initial state, the base policy can produce imprecise grasping or placement behaviors that lead to failure, while DIVER selects more appropriate actions and successfully completes the task.
